# 19 · What would make this matter

> **Project.** A reading of the whole repository on 2026-08-23: what runs, who it
> is for, what is genuinely different, what would kill it, and what to do next.
> **Nothing here is a new measurement.** Every number is either quoted from a
> document that recorded it or **[read]** off an artifact on disk, and the two
> places where that distinction bites are marked in §1. The one number this
> document changed is the published gate count, and it now has a check.

## The short version

1. **Three of the four pillars run; one does not exist.** `ai-flows` and `ai-ui`
   are real and tested. `ai-storage` is 0 lines of code and 1 specification.
2. **The strongest evidence in the repository is the part CI never runs.** There
   is no Python anywhere in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` **[read]**, so
   `projects/coclea-sr` — 28 gates, 135 checks — and `projects/hemo-verified`
   are outside every automated guard the repository has.
3. **And it had already rotted.** <!-- gate-count: superseded --> Thirteen claim sites across seven
   files said *26 gates / 125 checks* while the reports on disk held **28 / 135**. That is the
   exact failure `scripts/check-test-count.sh` was written to stop, applied to
   one number and not the next one.
4. **What is different is not the flow engine.** It is the rule that truth must
   be generated by something forbidden to import the code under test, plus a
   ledger that makes a result reproducible by a stranger, plus a habit of
   publishing the measurements that came back against the design.
5. **There is no user.** Two stars, one author, 54 commits in nineteen days. The
   plan in §6 is ordered by that fact rather than by the roadmap.

### The three numbers that had drifted

| claim | where it said | what the artifact says |
|---|---|---|
| gates / checks | <!-- gate-count: superseded --> **26 / 125**, in thirteen places across seven files | **28 / 135** in `projects/coclea-sr/gates/reports/` |

None of this is sloppiness in the ordinary sense. `doc/PLAN.md` had **28 / 135**
correct on the day it changed; the count simply lives in thirteen places and only one
number in this repository — the test count — had a script watching it. **A rule
applied to one number and not to the next one is a habit, not a check**, and a
habit is exactly what the repository's own house rule 4 says not to rely on.

Two of the three are closed in this change: `scripts/check-gate-count.py` reads
the reports, scans every markdown file for a published count in either language,
and fails on a mismatch, on a claim in a file nobody listed, and on a listed file
that has stopped stating one **[ran]** — plus a CI job so it runs on every PR.
`NEXT.md` is rewritten. The 605/626 case is *not* closed: `check-test-count.sh`
still looks at the two READMEs only, and extending it is [P0](#p0--stop-the-evidence-rotting) below.

### The asymmetry that matters most

`scripts/check-gate-count.py` verifies that the published count matches the
reports. **It cannot verify the reports are current**, because `make gates` is
nine minutes and needs numpy, scipy and sympy — which is precisely why nobody put
it in CI, and precisely why the count drifted.

So the repository is in this position: its cheapest evidence (TypeScript unit
tests) is guarded by nine CI jobs, and its most expensive evidence — the thing every
external claim rests on, the thing [18](18-from-a-hypothesis-to-a-therapeutic-surface.md)
is written about — is guarded by somebody remembering to run it. FRICTION F3 and
F8 are both instances of that same shape, found by hand, after the fact. The
plate at the top of this document is that sentence drawn.

## 2 · Who this is for, and why nobody is using it

**Cost to first value, today.** Postgres in Docker, a 1.31 GB `linux/amd64`
sandbox image (emulated, and therefore minutes per tool call, on Apple Silicon),
an OpenRouter key, Node ≥ 24.18, three processes, and a seed script. The
[manual](manual.md) is honest and complete about all of it, which makes the cost
legible rather than smaller. The only zero-cost path is
[the browser demo](https://evolvingagentslabs.github.io/demo/), and its backend is
simulated — it shows the interface, not the system.

**Distribution.** `ai-os` has 2 stars. The organisation's real distribution is
`evolving-agents` at 453, and it is archived under
[the freeze policy](07-freeze-policy.md). Every other repository in the
organisation is archived too. So the new project inherits the reputation and none
of the traffic.

**Therefore the honest statement is that the only user is the author**, and the
first genuine milestone in §6 that is not a measurement is *a second person gets
a gated result out*.

Three audiences are plausible. They are not equally supported by what is in the
repository:

**(a) The agent-platform builder** — wants flows, canvas, multiplayer handoff.
This is the [00-vision](00-vision.md) audience and it is the *weakest* fit today:
one flow shape runs, merge is unbuilt and honestly hard, the canvas has not been
falsified, and every well-funded product in the category is moving on the same
ground. Competing here on features is competing where ai-os has one author.

**(b) The person with a workload that has an external oracle** — physics,
numerics, simulation, anything where a right answer exists outside the model's
opinion. `coclea-sr` and `hemo-verified` are two worked instances, and
[16](16-a-workload-with-an-oracle.md) is the seam that made them possible. This
is where the repository has proof rather than argument.

**(c) The RL-environment and eval builder** — someone who needs reward computed
by something other than a judge. `projects/coclea-sr/environments/coclea_sr/` is
already shaped as an environment, and `physics-verifiers` is the sibling
repository that measured the argument.

**(b) and (c) are the same person often enough to be one audience**, and it is the
audience the evidence supports. Aiming at (a) first is what the last four
archived repositories in this organisation did.

## 3 · What is actually different

Five candidates, each stated with what it would take to dismiss it.

**1 · Truth that the code under test cannot produce.** `truth/` must not import
`src/`. Closed forms in sympy and mpmath on one side, the solver on the other, a
gate comparing them. Four words of policy, and they are the reason a green gate
means something other than self-consistency.
*Dismissed by:* showing the same discipline is standard practice. It is standard
in numerical analysis and it is *not* standard in agent systems, which is the
whole of the claim.

**2 · The gate report as a language-neutral kernel seam.** A Python process
writes JSON; `ai-flows/src/gates.ts` parses, summarises and decides, and executes
nothing. That is the answer an operating system is supposed to give to *"what
language is the work written in?"* — **none of the kernel's business**. It is
also the reason a metric could finally be declared for a step when every previous
workload ran on prose.
*Dismissed by:* a system that governs work across languages without a shared
format. Nobody has shown one; the alternative in practice is a judge model, which
is candidate 5's problem.

**3 · "Did not run" is not "passed".** `freezeVerdict` returns `blockers` and
`unknown` as separate lists and refuses on either. One design decision, three
lines of consequence, and it is the difference between a freeze gate that opens
widest when the suite is broken and one that does not.
*Dismissed by:* nothing — but it is small, and it is only load-bearing because
F3 and F8 both fired on exactly that boundary.

**4 · Reproduction as attestation, not as a claim.** Content-addressed run
directories, a hash-chained `ledger.jsonl`, run ids inside the PNGs' own
metadata, `verify_ledger.py` in stdlib only, and `make reproduce` checking each
re-run lands in its **existing** directory — same content, same hash, same path.
It caught its own store once, which is why F4 says "well enough" and not
"solved".
*Dismissed by:* showing it is ceremony. The counter-evidence is F8: two attested
`result.json` files were intact and not valid JSON, and `verify_ledger.py` found
it by refusing to conflate *intact* with *valid*.

**5 · A ledger of results that came back against the design.** The M4 benchmark
saturated. `dream` tied. The review study found nothing and its first draft's
finding was an artefact of one full stop. E7's arms all tied at the ceiling.
`evolving-memory` measured 80% against 80% and published anyway. And
`physics-verifiers` **falsified the usual argument for gates** — a frontier judge
caught twelve blatant fabrications and nine subtle numerical defects, twice.
*Dismissed by:* nobody, and this is the asset. It is also the reason the pitch in
§4 has to be the narrow one.

**What is not different.** The flow object is real and is one shape; the durable
unit of work is a good argument ([00-vision](00-vision.md)) with M2's evidence
behind it and not yet a differentiator a user can feel. The desk is built and
unproven. Scoped memory does not exist. Agents-as-markdown-files is a convention
several systems now share.

## 4 · Why the industry should care — stated narrowly

The bottleneck in agent systems is not producing output. It is knowing when the
output is wrong at a cost lower than producing it again. Every eval stack in wide
use answers that with a model, and this repository contains an experiment
saying a model answers it **well** — which is the inconvenient result, and the
one the pitch has to survive.

So the claim worth making outside is the narrow one, and it is the one
[18 §8](18-from-a-hypothesis-to-a-therapeutic-surface.md) already states:

- **A model can judge a task; it cannot generate one with a known answer.** You
  do not create truth by asserting it, however good the assertion is. `truth/`
  is a mechanism for having an answer nobody argued for.
- **A judge that is right every time still hands you no ledger, no freeze and no
  reproduction command.** Detection is not the same product as attestation. The
  second is what a regulator, a reviewer or a colleague six months later needs.
- **The kernel does not need to be written in the language of the work.** That is
  the operating-system claim, and it is the one with a running seam behind it.

What must not be claimed: that agents did science, that any statement here has
been compared against patient data, or that gates beat judges at detection. All
three are contradicted by measurements in this repository.

## 6 · The plan

Ordered by what is in the way, not by the roadmap. Every item states what done
means and what would say the item was the wrong one.

| | item | cost | why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Stop the evidence rotting | hours | the only asset, and nothing watches it |
| **P1** | Run M5's stopwatch | a person, and three days of waiting | the oldest unpaid debt; it can delete a pillar |
| **P2** | coclea §7.5 route B precondition | one run | unchanged from [PLAN](PLAN.md); it says whether route A is needed |
| **P3** | hemo H1 — the errors nobody designed | a surrogate, then the suite | the highest-value experiment in the repository |
| **P4** | One user who is not the author | a clean machine and a stopwatch | there is no evidence anything here is usable by a second person |
| **P5** | Say it once, narrowly | a day of writing | (b) and (c) from §2 cannot find this |
| **P6** | `ai-storage`, against 3.0 | a milestone | it is real work and it is behind five cheaper things |

### P0 · Stop the evidence rotting

Partly done in the change that carries this document: the gate count is checked
against the reports and wired into CI **[ran]**. What remains:

1. **Run the Python gates on a schedule.** Not per-PR — nine minutes, three
   scientific dependencies, and `projects/` changes rarely. A nightly or weekly
   job that runs `make gates` for both projects and fails loudly is enough, and
   it is the difference between a red gate being found by CI and being found by
   a document.
2. **Extend `check-test-count.sh` past the two READMEs**, since doc 18 carried
   605 while the READMEs carried 626.
3. **Run `gates/check_reports.py` in the same job**, because a fresh count over
   stale reports is the failure this whole section is about.

**Done means:** a red gate, a stale report and a drifted number each fail
something automatically. **Wrong item if:** the scheduled job proves too flaky or
too slow to keep green, in which case say so and pin a monthly attested run
instead of leaving a red badge people learn to ignore.

### P1 · Run M5's stopwatch

Unchanged from [04-ai-ui](04-ai-ui.md) and [NEXT](../NEXT.md): a person, a flow
**they did not run**, three days old, time to answer *what is the state, what is
blocked, what did it produce?* — desk against the `web-ui` transcript.

The flow has to be three days old, so **it is seeded first and measured later in
the week**, which is why this sits above items that look more urgent. Two
subjects is a signal about whether the instrument works, not evidence; the report
says which.

**Done means:** the number is published whichever way it comes out.
**Wrong item if:** it cannot be staffed with a second person at all — in which
case P4 is strictly prior, and the desk stays unproven with that stated on its
own document rather than left implied.

### P2 · coclea §7.5, route B, the precondition

Unchanged and not reordered. [PLAN](PLAN.md) argues it and the argument holds: it
is one run, and a run that says *route B cannot reach criticality* has saved a
milestone. The falsification is registered before the sweep is bought, the
regression at large `|mu_H|` must reproduce the passive curve, and the runner
returns non-zero if it does not.

This document has no standing to re-plan the thesis. It is here so the ordering
is visible against everything else.

### P3 · hemo-verified H1 — the errors nobody designed

`H0` survives at AUC 0.906, and its own README states the limit that matters:
**the corruptions and the oracles were designed by the same author.** H0 shows
the portfolio ranks errors somebody thought of. H1 is whether it ranks the errors
a trained surrogate actually makes.

This is the highest-value experiment in the repository, because it is the one
that can generalise the whole architecture argument past two hand-built
workloads — and because it can fail in a way that is worth publishing.

**Done means:** an AUC on surrogate errors, published whichever way it comes out,
next to H0's.
**Wrong item if:** training a surrogate turns out to be the milestone rather than
the precondition. Then the cheap version is to source real errors from an
existing published surrogate rather than to build one, and that decision is made
before the training is bought — F5, applied before the work.

### P4 · One user who is not the author

Two concrete things, and neither is a feature:

1. **`make up` from a clean clone on a clean machine, timed**, by somebody who
   has not seen the repository. Every failure becomes a FRICTION entry, fixed with
   the shortest hack that works. The target is a number, not an adjective: time to
   a first gated result.
2. **A third workload chosen by someone else.** Both existing projects were
   chosen by the person who built the seam, which is the same criticism H0 makes
   of itself.

**Done means:** a person who did not write it gets a gated result out, and the
elapsed time is recorded.
**Wrong item if:** time-to-first-value stays above an hour after the obvious
fixes. Then this is a private research instrument that publishes its findings,
which is a legitimate and much smaller thing to be — and the READMEs should say
so instead of saying "operating system".

### P5 · Say it once, narrowly

One artifact aimed at §2's audience (b)+(c): the `truth/`-cannot-import-`src/`
rule, the JSON gate seam, the attestation chain, and — prominently, not in a
footnote — the `physics-verifiers` result that killed the strong version of the
argument. A pitch that leads with its own strongest counter-evidence is the only
kind this repository is entitled to make, and it is also the one that is hard to
dismiss.

**Done means:** published, with the narrow claims of §4 and none of the wide
ones.
**Wrong item if:** it draws attention before P0 and P1 are done, and the first
serious reader finds a stale number or an unproven canvas. That is the failure
mode the freeze policy exists to prevent, arriving from the other direction.

### P6 · `ai-storage`, against 3.0

M4 proceeds — the gate opened on `long-horizon-eviction` — but the two caveats
travel with the number: one fixture is not a benchmark, and the judge is deepseek
grading deepseek's own summariser. So the first move is still the one
[NEXT](../NEXT.md) named: **a second long-horizon fixture written to a different
shape, by a different hand**, and the open question answered on paper against two
real flows — *when two notes say the same thing, which survives?* — before any
store is built.

It is last because it is a milestone and the five items above it are hours, one
run, and a person.

### What not to do

Unchanged from [08-roadmap § Deliberately not planned](08-roadmap.md), plus three
that follow from this document:

- **No more desk before the stopwatch.** `gate-face.ts` was built while H9 was
  still unexecuted, and [PLAN](PLAN.md) records that as the pattern FRICTION.md
  exists to replace.
- **No third project before a second user.** A third workload chosen by the same
  author is a third instance of the same fixture.
- **Do not restate the gate argument in the strong form.** "The model cannot
  tell" is measured false, twice, by this organisation.
